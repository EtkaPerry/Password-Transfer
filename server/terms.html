4. What the Service does

Password Transfer lets you move a short piece of text, a password, or up to four images from one device (the "sender") to another (the "receiver") without typing it out, using a QR code that is displayed on the receiving device and scanned by the sending device.

  1. The receiving device generates a random session identifier and a random encryption key, and displays them together as a QR code. The key is contained in the URL fragment, which browsers do not normally transmit to a server.
  2. The sending device scans the code, encrypts your content in your browser using AES-256-GCM, and uploads only the resulting ciphertext to our server against that session identifier.
  3. The receiving device fetches the ciphertext and decrypts it locally. The stored ciphertext is deleted from the server on first retrieval, and in any event expires shortly afterwards.

We do not hold the decryption key, and we therefore cannot read, recover, moderate or restore the content of a transfer. This is a deliberate design property of the Service, and it has consequences described in sections 7, 8 and 15.

7. Security — what we do and what we cannot promise

Encryption and decryption happen in your browser using the Web Crypto API (AES-256-GCM), with keys generated by your browser's cryptographic random number generator. Displayed QR codes rotate periodically, unclaimed sessions expire, and stored ciphertext is deleted on first retrieval.

You should nevertheless understand the following limits, which we state openly rather than leave implied:

  • Security depends on the integrity of both devices and browsers. A compromised phone or computer — including one with a keylogger, screen recorder or malicious extension — can capture your content before encryption or after decryption. The Service reduces typing-related exposure; it does not make an untrusted computer safe.
  • The decryption key travels in the URL fragment specifically so that it is not sent to our server. Some mobile camera and messaging apps rewrite a fragment into a query string when opening a link, and a query string is transmitted in the request — so in that situation the key reaches our server, our hosting provider and any network intermediary, and may be recorded in their logs. The Service now detects this: the key is removed from the address immediately, the Service does not send a referrer containing it, and you are shown a warning and asked to scan the QR code again instead of continuing. Because the request carrying the key arrives before any of our code can run, we cannot undo that first disclosure — we can only stop it spreading further and let you decide. If you choose to continue past that warning, treat the transfer as one we could in principle read.
  • No online service can be guaranteed secure. We do not warrant that the Service is free of vulnerabilities or that a transfer cannot be intercepted or compromised.

If you believe you have found a security vulnerability, please report it privately to legal@your-domain.example before disclosing it publicly, and do not access, alter or retain other people's data while investigating.
